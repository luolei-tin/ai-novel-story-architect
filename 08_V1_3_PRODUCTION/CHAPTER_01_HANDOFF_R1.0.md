## Chapter Objective

让读者看到周衡第一次面对“重生优势”与“现实现金约束”之间的冲突，并让他主动选择旧商场的180天经营权。

本章不是介绍世界观。
本章必须让一个商业决定真正发生。

## Starting State

- Time: ten years before the main story's original timeline.
- Zhou Heng has just returned to this period.
- Available liquid cash: 30,000 RMB.
- He possesses only partial memories of future trends.
- He does not possess reliable exact dates for local events.
- The old mall has 27 empty shops and several surviving merchants.
- A 180-day operating-and-leasing opportunity exists.
- Performance deposit required: 20,000 RMB.

## Zhou Heng — Character Intent

Want:
keep enough liquidity to avoid repeating the helplessness of his previous life.

Fear:
making one irreversible commitment and losing all fallback options.

Knowledge:
the surrounding district will improve in the future; the mall itself will later become more valuable.

Unknown:
exact timing of local changes; exact tenant survival; exact financing conditions; the complete chain of consequences.

Contradiction:
he tells himself that liquidity is safety, but emotionally he is tempted to secure control of the asset first.

## Other Character Intent

Seller/Creditor representative:
want a credible operator who can produce verifiable operating results before the next disposal step.

Lin Zhiyi:
appears only briefly or indirectly in this chapter. She is not yet Zhou's ally. Her function is to establish that merchant trust is a live local variable.

## Causal Chain

1. Zhou verifies the mall's operating-right opportunity.
2. He confirms the 20,000 RMB performance deposit.
3. He compares this with at least one future-knowledge-based alternative that has higher upside but uncertain timing.
4. He calculates the remaining 10,000 RMB liquidity and recognizes that this is barely a survivable buffer.
5. He inspects the mall and encounters evidence that the opportunity is not simply an empty building: surviving merchants and the nearby market's time pressure matter.
6. A representative makes it clear that future appreciation is irrelevant to the contract; present operating performance matters.
7. Zhou chooses the operating right.
8. The deposit is committed.

## Decision Requirement

The choice must be reversible only in a limited sense.
Once the deposit is committed, Zhou cannot casually return to his original 30,000 RMB position.

The dramatic point is not that he knows the mall will become valuable.
The dramatic point is that he is deliberately choosing a narrow, survivable exposure over an easier speculative story.

## Information Control

Reader knows:
- Zhou has knowledge of a future that others do not.
- He has very little current capital.
- The mall appears distressed.

Zhou knows:
- broad future appreciation;
- the current contract terms;
- his own cash limit.

Other characters know:
- the mall is distressed;
- Zhou is new and capital-light;
- his credibility is unproven.

Hidden:
- the exact future value of the parcel;
- the exact future path of the district;
- the eventual role of Lin and Zhao.

## Forbidden Moves

Do not:
- make Zhou quote exact future property prices as certainty;
- make him know exact construction dates or tenant outcomes;
- make the creditor trust him immediately;
- let him solve the entire mall problem in one conversation;
- introduce Zhao as a villain;
- explain his whole psychological arc through internal monologue;
- have characters state the thesis directly;
- use accidental money, lottery winnings or unexplained help to solve the capital problem;
- make the mall instantly attractive to every merchant;
- skip the cash calculation.

## Foreshadowing

F01 candidate plant:
a seemingly boring clause about verified collections and operating evidence.

F02 candidate plant:
Lin Zhiyi is associated with a merchant saying that promises must be payable before they are believable.

F03 candidate plant:
the creditor representative emphasizes that operating rights and ownership are separate.

These should appear naturally. Do not label them as foreshadowing.

## Ending State

Mandatory state deltas:
- Zhou's liquid cash: 30,000 → 10,000 RMB.
- Operating-right decision: not chosen → committed.
- Personal risk: low → materially exposed.
- Relationship with mall stakeholders: unknown → cautiously conditional.
- Zhou's psychological behavior: speculative temptation → first deliberate downside constraint.

End-of-chapter hook:
Zhou enters the empty mall after the contract is committed and notices that the first problem is not finding a valuable future asset. It is getting three real people to believe that he can keep a promise with only 10,000 RMB left.
