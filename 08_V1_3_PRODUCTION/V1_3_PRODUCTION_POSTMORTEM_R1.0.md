# V1.3 Production Postmortem — R1.0

## Verdict

The first four chapter prose experiment is a structural-quality failure despite passing the original logic/continuity checks.

This is an important result.

The system detected:
- causal inconsistency in Chapter 4;
- financial continuity issues;
- information integrity;
- world-rule consistency.

But it failed to reject the deeper problem:

> the prose was functioning as an expanded outline.

## Primary Failure

The prose repeatedly followed:

event
→ explanation
→ protagonist calculation
→ protagonist states lesson
→ decision
→ chapter summary.

This made the business plot legible but insufficiently dramatic.

## Why The Existing Auditor Missed It

The original Chapter Auditor focused on:
- causality;
- character consistency;
- information;
- structure;
- continuity;
- foreshadowing.

It did not explicitly test:
- opposing objectives inside scenes;
- tactical conflict;
- reversals;
- subtext;
- independent NPC objectives;
- whether dialogue changes power or choice;
- whether chapter endings generate the next problem rather than summarize the lesson.

Therefore a logically correct flowchart could pass.

## Specific Symptoms in Chapters 1–4

### Chapter 1

The protagonist's choice is clear, but too much of the chapter is retrospective explanation of his previous failure and present philosophy.

### Chapter 2

The supplier scene provides a clean decision, but the business conflict is closer to a financial exercise than a human confrontation.

The neighbor scene then repeats the same idea rather than creating a new dramatic turn.

### Chapter 3

The tenant conflict is more active, but the other characters still primarily serve the protagonist's lesson.

The protagonist explains the trade-off to himself after the choice, reducing reader inference.

### Chapter 4

The contract conflict becomes more concrete, but the dialogue frequently states the governing principle instead of letting competing interests reveal it through behavior.

The original draft also contained a real continuity/causality defect and was correctly REJECTED.

## Architectural Correction

The system must now distinguish:

### Logical Validity

Does the story make sense?

### Dramatic Validity

Does the story generate conflict, choice, reversal, cost, and changed relationships through character pursuit?

Both are required for PASS.

## New Rule

A chapter that is logically correct but reads like an event list with explanations must not receive PASS.

It should receive REVISE or REJECT depending on severity.

## Canon Status

Chapters 1–4 remain useful as diagnostic artifacts.

They are not the quality benchmark and should not be treated as the canonical prose standard for the novel.

The next production pass should redesign the dramatic architecture before rewriting prose.
