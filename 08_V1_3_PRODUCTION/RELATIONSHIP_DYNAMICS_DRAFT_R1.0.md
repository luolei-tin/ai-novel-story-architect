# Relationship Dynamics Draft — R1.0

## Zhou Heng ↔ Lin Zhiyi

### Initial State

Zhou Heng sees Lin Zhiyi as useful but too conservative.

Lin Zhiyi sees Zhou Heng as unusually perceptive but does not yet trust his promises.

Their leverage is different:
- Zhou controls available cash and final business decisions.
- Lin controls tenant relationships and operating information.

### Mutual Need

Zhou needs Lin to turn plans into actual tenant collections.

Lin needs Zhou to set limits he will actually honor instead of making promises he cannot fund.

### Information Gap

Zhou has broad memories of the future.

Lin has detailed knowledge of present merchants, leases and local behavior.

Neither has the other's complete information.

### First Irreversible Event

Zhou wants to reject a weak-looking tenant.

Lin keeps the tenant with a strictly bounded concession.

That tenant later becomes the first stable cash-generating anchor.

Zhou must change a real operating rule from:

"highest immediate rent"

to:

"highest probability of stable collection."

### Core Conflict

When cash becomes tight, Zhou wants to cut a merchant support promise.

Lin argues that breaking it will damage the trust network that produces future collections.

The conflict has measurable business consequences.

### Relationship Arc

utility
→ tentative respect
→ conflict over promises
→ shared loss
→ bounded trust
→ interdependence
→ governance conflict
→ institutionalized partnership.

### Delete Test

Remove Lin:
- the first merchant anchor loses its causal origin;
- tenant trust becomes generic exposition;
- Zhou's future knowledge cannot fully replace local relationship information;
- the final governance choice loses part of its operational and emotional cost.

Current result:
Lin passes the non-substitutability candidate test, pending chapter evidence.

## Zhou Heng ↔ Zhao Qisheng

### Initial State

Zhao does not initially regard Zhou as a serious competitor.

Zhou initially regards Zhao as the eventual opponent because he remembers the operator's later expansion.

This creates an information asymmetry:
Zhou knows Zhao's possible future importance; Zhao only sees a small new operator.

### Conflict Rule

Zhao will cooperate whenever cooperation increases expected gain and compete whenever Zhou threatens his control or acquisition path.

He is not allowed to oppose Zhou merely because the plot needs an antagonist.

### Required Irreversible Relationship Turn

There must be one event where Zhou chooses not to eliminate Zhao when he has a realistic opportunity to do so.

That choice later changes the form of their conflict.

The reason cannot be moral preaching. It must be a business decision with a cost.
