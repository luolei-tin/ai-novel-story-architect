# Chapter 04 Red-Team Audit — R1.0

Artifact: CHAPTER_04_DRAFT_R1.0
Upstream: CHAPTER_04_HANDOFF_R1.0
Verdict: REVISE

## FATAL / HIGH

### HIGH — The Chapter's Contract Logic Is Internally Inconsistent

The handoff says Tenant B agrees to sign if Zhou handles basic electrical and wall repairs before opening, with a repair cap and a written loading schedule.

The prose first has the tenant nearly withdraw because Zhou insists on strict written limits. Later, an electrical failure occurs and Zhou pays 380 RMB before the contract is signed.

This creates an unresolved question:
Was the 380 RMB repair obligation part of the signed agreement, an emergency landlord duty, or a voluntary pre-contract expense?

The current chapter does not establish the contractual basis.

### Why It Matters

The story's central rule W5 is that contracts define real boundaries.

The chapter cannot simultaneously make the contract the solution and leave the most concrete expenditure outside the contract without explanation.

### Minimum Repair

Choose one explicit causal path:

A. Sign the contract first with the 1,000 RMB repair cap, then the 380 RMB repair occurs as an agreed first repair.

or

B. Make the 380 RMB a documented emergency pre-contract repair that Zhou chooses to fund in order to preserve the tenant relationship, with the exact reason it does not bind him to the later recurring obligations.

Do not leave it ambiguous.

## MEDIUM — Tenant's Return Is Convenient

The tenant leaves after refusing the strict terms, then returns only shortly after the electrical problem.

This can work, but the return needs a concrete reason beyond "the light came back on."

Minimum repair:
Tie the return to a specific observation:
the tenant sees Zhou actually spending within a stated limit and keeping his word, which changes his risk assessment.

## MEDIUM — Zhou's Psychological Insight Is Too Fast

The line that Zhou fears loss of control is strong, but he reaches the explicit self-diagnosis almost immediately.

The deeper arc requires behavioral accumulation.

Minimum repair:
keep the thought less definitive here. Let the pattern become undeniable only after later failures.

## Continuity Risk

If the chapter is revised so that the 380 RMB is paid under contract, ending liquid cash becomes approximately 9,620 RMB.

That amount must be used in Chapter 5 onward.

## Verdict

REVISE.

Do not proceed to Chapter 5 until the contract/repair causal chain is repaired and continuity state is updated.
