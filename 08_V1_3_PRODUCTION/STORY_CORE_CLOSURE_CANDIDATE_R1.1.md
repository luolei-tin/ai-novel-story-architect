## 1. Why This Mall Is Necessary

The old mall is not valuable merely because Zhou Heng remembers its later appreciation.

It contains a rare four-part conjunction that exists only during a short window:
1. Distressed operating right — the creditor is willing to grant a 180-day operating-and-leasing mandate before the asset enters its next disposal stage.
2. Low entry capital — the performance deposit is 20,000 RMB; Zhou Heng has 30,000 RMB. He cannot afford to buy property, but he can afford this narrow operating right.
3. Time-locked tenant source — the nearby wholesale market is scheduled for demolition in 90 days. A group of small merchants needs a nearby, low-commitment relocation venue.
4. Existing infrastructure — the mall already has basic mixed-use electrical load, loading access and customer parking needed by that tenant pool. Competing vacant properties nearby would require capital and approvals Zhou cannot fund.

Therefore future appreciation alone does not create the opportunity.
The opportunity exists because distressed operating right + 30k entry threshold + 90-day tenant displacement + usable infrastructure overlap for a limited period.

## 2. Counterfactual — Refuse the Mall

If Zhou Heng refuses:
- the 20,000 RMB operating-right window expires;
- the nearby market merchants are absorbed by other operators;
- he retains 10,000 RMB but has no comparable low-capital operating asset;
- he can still start a different small business, but not one that simultaneously tests stable tenant collection, trust-based leasing and cash-flow management at this scale.

Therefore refusal does not make the novel impossible, but it changes the core experiment.
The mall is necessary because it is the only available training ground that fits his capital, timing and intended operating model simultaneously.

## 3. Why Zhou Heng Knows It Is Worth Trying

Future knowledge tells him the surrounding district will improve and the parcel will become more valuable later.
But he does not know the exact operating path.
His decision must therefore be known long-term upside + current low entry cost + survivable downside, rather than I know it will rise so I cannot lose.

## 4. First Real Commercial Test

Zhou Heng's first target is not to fill all 27 shops.
He needs the first three tenants to prove:
- real foot traffic can be built;
- collections can become predictable;
- tenant mix improves each other;
- promises made by the operator can actually be delivered.

This produces a measurable operating loop:
tenant selection → promise → opening → customer flow → collection → renewal probability.
The first stable cash-flow proof must precede leverage.

## 5. First Failure Zhou Heng Cannot Solve Alone

Thirty days into the operation, one anchor merchant demands an electrical upgrade and extended loading access.
Zhou can pay for a limited modification, but the request conflicts with another tenant's existing usage and the mall's capacity limits.
His instinct is to negotiate directly with both tenants and impose a compromise.
He fails because both merchants distrust a new operator whose promises have not survived a cash shortage.
Lin Zhiyi can solve the relationship part because she previously worked with both merchant communities and understands which promise is actually non-negotiable.

Her intervention is not free:
- she demands that Zhou put the compromise in writing;
- she refuses to promise compensation that the company cannot fund;
- she risks her own reputation if the arrangement breaks.

## 6. Commercial Meaning

The first lesson is not trust Lin because she is good.
It is that current relationships contain information and leverage that future knowledge cannot replace.
This lesson later becomes structurally relevant to the final governance conflict.

## 7. Implication for Final Choice

The ending cannot be decided in the outline by author preference.
The middle must establish a repeated pattern:
Zhou controls more → short-term efficiency improves → hidden system fragility increases.
Bounded shared control → local decisions become slower → resilience and trust improve.
Only if this pattern is demonstrated several times can the final capital/governance decision become causally necessary.

## 8. Closure Status

The mall-necessity problem is now provisionally closed at the concept level.
It still requires outline evidence.
The final-choice problem remains OPEN until the middle structure demonstrates that control and resilience repeatedly trade against each other.
