## Chapter 1 — 拍卖名单上的旧商场

Zhou discovers the 180-day operating-right window.

He has 30,000 RMB.

The performance deposit is 20,000 RMB.

He explicitly compares two alternatives:
- a remembered future opportunity with high upside but uncertain timing;
- the mall with low immediate upside but survivable downside.

Choice:
He takes the mall operating right.

Cost:
He leaves himself only 10,000 RMB of liquid cash.

State change:
He is no longer merely a man with future knowledge. He is a small operator with almost no margin for error.

## Chapter 2 — 未来记得大概，现金必须算清

Zhou builds a 90-day cash budget.

He remembers that the surrounding district will improve "before summer", but cannot reliably remember the exact roadwork timing.

A supplier offers discounted renovation materials if Zhou prepays 4,000 RMB.

Zhou wants the discount because it could improve the first storefronts.

Choice:
He rejects the discount and preserves liquidity.

Reason:
His memory is insufficient to justify a fixed-date traffic bet.

Cost:
He pays a higher unit price later.

State change:
The first visible proof that he values liquidity over apparent margin.

## Chapter 3 — 最高的租金

Two potential tenants compete for one visible storefront.

Tenant A offers higher monthly rent and three months' rent upfront, but demands:
- 8,000 RMB renovation support;
- category exclusivity;
- flexible loading hours.

Tenant B offers lower rent and no upfront premium, but fits the surrounding merchants.

Zhou prefers A because the cash arrives earlier.

Lin argues that A's loading demand will collide with an existing tenant and the mall's limited capacity.

Choice:
Zhou chooses B.

Cost:
He gives up the most attractive immediate rent and 3 months of prepayment.

State change:
The business model is visibly tested against short-term temptation.

## Chapter 4 — 承诺要写进纸里

Three merchants are ready to sign.

Lin insists that two concessions be written with hard caps:
- renovation support;
- delivery/loading window.

Choice:
Zhou initially wants verbal flexibility to close faster, then accepts the written caps because he cannot afford ambiguous future liabilities.

Cost:
The contracts take two extra days, and one tenant almost walks.

State change:
Contract governance becomes part of Zhou's operating system.

## Chapter 5 — 开门第一周

The traffic experiment costs 1,200 RMB.

Instead of repainting the entire facade, Zhou spends on:
- directional signs;
- a low-cost local promotion;
- a one-week merchant sampling event.

The first week collects less than expected.

A merchant complains that delivery vehicles are blocking the front during the same period.

Earlier planted fact:
The mall has one usable loading window because of limited access.

Choice:
Zhou must decide whether to violate the written loading cap to protect current revenue.

He does not.

Cost:
One merchant loses a peak-hour delivery opportunity.

State change:
The contract constraint now produces real pain rather than decorative paperwork.

## Chapter 6 — 账上有收入，不等于有现金

First-month accounting looks acceptable, but collections arrive unevenly.

Projected monthly tenant collections:
- Tenant B: 3,000
- Merchant 2: 2,600
- Merchant 3: 2,400
Total: 8,000 RMB

Operating cash costs:
- utilities/security: 2,200
- agreed support: 1,500
- traffic/operations: 1,200
- repairs/incidentals: 1,000
Total: 5,900 RMB

Nominal operating surplus:
2,100 RMB.

But one tenant pays nine days late.

Choice:
Zhou delays an optional facade improvement rather than borrow short-term money.

State change:
Liquidity management becomes concrete.

## Chapter 7 — 电箱问题

Tenant B's sales increase.

It requests:
- an electrical upgrade;
- an expanded loading window.

The request conflicts with another tenant's written loading slot and the mall's electrical capacity.

Earlier setup:
Ch03 established the loading conflict.
Ch04 established the contractual cap.
Ch05 demonstrated the cap's operational cost.

Choice:
Zhou tries to redesign the schedule himself and offers a compensation package.

He misjudges which promise the merchant considers essential.

Result:
Neither side accepts the compromise.

State change:
Zhou's direct-control strategy fails for a reason prepared by earlier chapters.

## Chapter 8 — 林知微不肯替他兜底

Zhou asks Lin to mediate.

Lin refuses to make a new promise on Zhou's behalf.

Her condition:
Zhou must choose which commitment the business can actually fund, put the result in writing, and accept the loss created for the other tenant.

Choice:
Zhou gives Lin authority over the merchant-negotiation process within that financial boundary.

Cost:
For the first time he lets another person control an important part of an outcome.

Result:
A temporary compromise is reached.

State change:
Lin becomes a bounded partner, not an employee executing orders.

## Chapter 9 — 第一个稳定月

The three-tenant cluster reaches:
- collections: 8,700 RMB;
- recurring operating cash costs: 5,600 RMB;
- operating cash contribution: 3,100 RMB.

The result is not an unexplained success.

Causal bridge:
- Tenant B created reliable demand;
- Lin prevented a trust-breaking promise;
- Zhou maintained liquidity;
- the traffic experiment generated repeat customers;
- the loading dispute was contained rather than ignored.

Choice:
Zhou writes the first tenant-selection and support rules.

State change:
He begins converting personal judgment into a repeatable system.

## Chapter 10 — 一个不急着赚钱的人

An unnamed commercial operator has been acquiring nearby distressed leases and prioritizing control of future tenant flow.

A broker explains the operator's model without naming its leader.

Later Zhou learns the operator is Zhao Qisheng.

Zhao offers a limited cooperation arrangement:
he can introduce a tenant with strong traffic but wants influence over the mall's future expansion.

Choice:
Zhou does not immediately reject Zhao.

Reason:
The tenant could improve current cash flow, while Zhou wants to understand Zhao's capital model.

Cost:
Zhou gives Zhao a small amount of information about the mall's operating metrics.

State change:
Rivalry becomes a rational commercial relationship.

## Chapter 11 — 九十天倒计时

The nearby wholesale market demolition date is confirmed.

A larger group of merchants now needs relocation.

Zhou could rapidly fill the mall.

But the new package requires:
- rent-free periods;
- renovation commitments;
- cash advances;
- more security deposits.

Choice:
Zhou refuses the full package and accepts only the tenants whose downside can be funded.

Cost:
He knowingly leaves significant revenue on the table.

Lin supports the decision, but warns that another operator may absorb the rejected merchants.

State change:
Zhou's survivability principle is tested against genuine opportunity cost.

## Chapter 12 — 不是所有的钱都该拿

Zhao's capital partner proposes a 50,000 RMB working-capital line.

Terms include:
- broad personal guarantee;
- approval over major renovation spending;
- first right to participate in a future equity transaction.

The money would allow Zhou to take the larger merchant package.

Choice:
Zhou rejects the line.

This is not because debt is universally bad.

It is because the financing structure creates a control obligation before the business has sufficient evidence or governance.

Cost:
Growth slows; Zhao gains room to compete for the displaced merchants.

State change:
The arc closes with stable cash flow but deliberately constrained growth.

## Arc Evidence Requirements

To reach the next arc, the prose must visibly establish:

1. the loading constraint before Ch07;
2. the written promise before the promise is later tested;
3. the 8,000+ monthly collection path rather than a narrated "business is improving";
4. Zhou's first real delegation of authority;
5. Zhao's cooperation offer as economically rational;
6. a measurable opportunity cost from refusing debt.
