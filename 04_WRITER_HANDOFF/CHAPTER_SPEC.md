# Chapter Specification — Revised

The Writer Handoff is not a mini-outline of prose events.

It is a dramatic contract.

## Required Input

### 1. Chapter Objective
What irreversible story function must this chapter perform?

### 2. Starting State
Time, place, physical condition, resources, knowledge, relationships, unresolved pressure.

### 3. Dramatic Situation
At least one character wants something now, and another person or force has a legitimate reason to resist.

State explicitly:
- POV objective;
- opposing objective;
- stakes;
- leverage;
- information asymmetry.

### 4. Causal Spine
Only the minimum causal beats:
- trigger;
- pressure;
- tactical escalation;
- reversal;
- consequential choice;
- immediate consequence;
- new state.

Do not prescribe every scene.

### 5. Character Pressure
For each key character:
- Want now;
- Fear now;
- what they will not admit;
- current tactic;
- threshold that forces a new tactic.

### 6. Information Control
Reader knows / protagonist knows / other characters know / hidden.

### 7. Relationship Movement
What each person believes about the other at start.
What choice or revelation changes that belief.
What new behavior results.

### 8. Forbidden Moves
No unauthorized restructuring, no theme speeches, no omniscience, no convenient coincidence, no sudden psychology change, no business result without causal support.

### 9. Ending State
The chapter must end on a changed situation, not merely a conclusion of thought.

The next problem should be generated by the chapter's choice whenever possible.

## Writer Freedom

The writer controls:
- wording;
- scene order within the causal spine;
- sensory detail;
- dialogue style;
- pacing;
- POV texture.

The writer does not control:
- core causal choice;
- character psychology;
- world rules;
- required state transition.

## Anti-Flowchart Rule

Do not construct a chapter as:

event → explanation → calculation → lesson → conclusion.

The chapter must be experienced through conflict, tactics, behavior, and consequence.
